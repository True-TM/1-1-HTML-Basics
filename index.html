Monologues

(From a drama student)

Monologues are chunks of text that an actor reads as the given character. Monologues are said to either one and more people, which is what differenciates it from a soliloquy, which is a chunk of text that an actor reads as the given character to themself. Soliloques are purely for oneself.

An example monologue

SOPHIE: I wish—I wish I was more… Like you. Like you. You’re so – you’re so clear. You seem so clear about things. Whereas I’m – I’m so – I can never quite say what I’m – Even to myself, I’m so inarticulate. [Beat] Some nights I lie awake and go over the things I’ve said. Confidently. The things I’ve said confidently and they – they fall to pieces. [Beat] And where there were words there is now just – just this feeling of – of impossibility. That everything is – There’s no way through it – [Beat] I – used to feel that way when I – was very small. That same feeling. Not a childish feeling – well maybe. As if I was choking on – As if life was coming down on me and I couldn’t see my way through it. What does a child who has everything suffer from? Who could name it? I can’t. I can't. [Breaking] But it – was a -sort of – I used to see it in my head as a jungle. Around me. Surrounding me. Some darkness growing, something – organic, alive – and the only thing that kept me – kept me – here – was the picture of Honour and George. Silly. [Beat] But I still feel – I need – I need… [Beat] I wish – I – wish I was more – like you. Like you.

Signs of a strong monologue:

  • There's a strong objective
  • You have a clear POV
  • The character is close to self
  • You're able to 'drop in'
